Rules for TAs


Preparing for the quarter

In preparation to TA FISH 406, TAs should do the following:

  • take the appropriate training to obtain access the UW vivarium (talk to Chelsea about how to get this started)
  • familiarize yourself with all course content, including readings and videos
  • read Combes’ Parasitism: The Ecology and Evolution of Intimate Interactions
  • read Karban, Huntzinger, and Pearse’s How to Do Ecology: A Concise Handbook
  • read Roberts and Janovy’s Foundations of Parasitology
  • work with the SAFS Facilities Manager to move all equipment and supplies into the teaching lab
  • check over the stockpile of teaching supplies and let Chelsea know if we are running low on anything
  • prepare living materials for the first few labs (especially the living schistosome-infected mice that are used in Lab 1)
  • set up the lab so that it is ready for students (e.g., arrange benches, microscopes, dissection tools, slides)
  • tune up the microscopes so that they are ready for students (e.g., clean optics, tighten screws)
  • check the agreement between the course website and Canvas site, especially screening for date discrepancies
  • consider whether and which class sessions you would like to lead, and let Chelsea know


Office hours

Each TA is required to hold one regularly scheduled 60-minute office hour per week. Before the quarter starts, please tell Chelsea when you’d like to hold your office hour, and she will update the course website accordingly. If you must cancel your office hour (e.g., due to illness), you are required to hold a make-up office hour as soon as possible thereafter. When students are not available during regularly scheduled office hours, TAs are required to make themselves available for meetings by appointment.


Instructional personnel meetings

FISH 406 TAs are required to make themselves available for weekly 30-minute instructional personnel meetings, where we prepare for the coming week’s labs, events, and assignments. Usually, these meetings are held in the 30 minutes following class every Friday (1:30-2pm).


Travel

TAing FISH 406 is a commitment and we take our responsibility to students very seriously. FISH 406 TAs are therefore not permitted to engage, during the quarter of their TAship, in any travel that would interfere with their ability to attend lecture, lab sessions, or regularly scheduled office hours. This includes field work and conference travel in addition to personal travel. No vacation or holiday time may be taken when classes are in session during the quarter of a FISH 406 TAship. Outside of the weeks when classes are in session, vacation and holiday time requests will be approved only if these requests do not interfere with FISH 406 commitments (e.g., preparing laboratory space for the quarter, grading final exams). This allows the instructional staff of the course to dedicate the time and attention that our students deserve.


Grading

In FISH 406, TAs are empowered to contribute to grading (e.g., lab notebooks, exams), but final decision-making authority rests with the instructor of record (Chelsea). TAs are expected:

  • to be familiar with all course policies, especially those that pertain to grading,
  • to avoid misinforming students about these policies, and
  • to point students to these policies when needed.

TAs are strictly forbidden from changing student grades without first consulting Chelsea; if it is discovered that a TA has changed a grade without first consulting Chelsea, Canvas gradebook privileges will be removed from all TAs and the TA who broke the rule will become ineligible for future FISH 406 TAships.


Norming

One of the most important duties of a FISH 406 TA is to provide feedback to students on their work: on exams, independent research projects, and presentations. To make sure that we are providing equal levels of support across students and grading by the same standards, the FISH 406 instructional team will periodically engage in “norming” exercises. When we “norm” our feedback for a given assignment, we will all get together to evaluate the same few assignments, compare our feedback, and discuss how to bring our feedback into alignment. We continue doing this until the instructor of record (Chelsea) is satisfied that every member of the instructional team is on the same page. This may be repeated for each exam/assignment.


How to order mice

  • All animal imports are handled by the Rodent Health Monitoring Program / Animal Purchasing in the Department of Comparative Medicine.

  • All requests must be submitted through the online system AOps (which is administered via Hoverboard) - step-by-step instructions are here.

  • Do you already have a registration for BEI Resources? You will need this to order the mice. If not, start this as early as possible - it is a long process that requires many documents.

  • Start by touching base with Margaret Mentink-Kane at BRI (mmentinkkane@afbr-bri.org).

    • Tell her what you want to do ahead of time to give her a heads-up.
    • Ask her for the latest BRI health reports (see below).
    • Figure out the timing. Mice should be six weeks post-infection (five weeks post-receipt at UW) when they are euthanized. That means that if your lab is scheduled for 6 Oct, the mice should be infected on 25 Aug and shipped on 1 Sep. The IACUC protocol says that the mice should be euthanized within 5 weeks of receipt, so stick to that. The earlier you euthanize, the fewer/smaller worms. The later you euthanize, the sicker the mice get, and the likelier they are to suffer and die. Five weeks post-receipt seems like the right balance.

How to care for and euthanize mice

  1. Mice will be shipped by BRI and should arrive at the Foege loading dock, where they will be received by vivarium staff.Staff will then house the mice and indicate the room and rack in Hoverboard.

  2. Don’t count on getting a notification when the mice arrive. Check Hoverboard to find out whether they have made it or not and follow up accordingly. Note that only the person who actually places the mouse order (i.e., Chelsea) can check this in Hoverboard, and usually this same person will receive the tracking information from BRI / the freight company.

  3. As soon as mice arrive in the vivarium, our IACUC protocol requires that we check on them. Vivarium staff will check on the general well-being of the animals, but we are responsible for making sure that they are not exhibiting any signs of severe intestinal schistosomiasis:

  • body condition
  • respiratory effort
  • hair coat
  • activity
  • fecal output
  1. How to enter the vivarium:
  • Make sure that you are wearing close-toed shoes and long pants.
  • Use your Husky card to enter the Foege main door, then take the elevator to B.
  • Use your Husky card to enter the main door of the vivarium (to your left when you exit the elevator) and the secondary door of the vivarium (to your left as you pass through the main door).
  • Take a pair of scrubs from the rack.
  • Proceed to the locker rooms (down the hallway to your left as you enter the vivarium).
  • Take off your street clothes (underwear/undershirt/socks okay to leave on) and place them in one of the lockers.
  • Put on scrubs.
  • Proceed through the airlock.
  • Put on a hair net and sanitary booties.
  • Open the door on the other side and select a pair of crocs from the rack.
  • Proceed to the room where the mice are housed.
  • Put in your personal 4-digit code to access the room.
  • If your pin doesn’t work, consult Olena, whose office is located near the clean cages (as you enter the rodent side of the vivarium, turn right out of the locker rooms and right at the clean cages, and you’ll see her office in front of you).

  1. Mice should be checked once per week in the first three weeks and twice per week in the last two weeks.

  2. Check the mice using these criteria:

  • body condition
  • respiratory effort
  • hair coat
  • activity
  • fecal output

  1. If any mice appear to be in distress, alert the vivarium veterinarian immediately.

  2. How to euthanize the mice:

  • Remember to bring a small hand-held cooler with you. This can be placed in the ante room as you enter the vivarium (the room to the left of the doors to the locker rooms).
  • Remove the cages from the rack and carry them to an empty procedure room. Any room with a hood and a cage lid equipped with a port for CO2 will do.
  • If you only want to euthanize a subset of the mice in a given cage, remember that the survivors shouldn’t watch the euthanasia take place. Grab a clean cage from the rack in front of the vivarium offices (the hallway to your right as you exit the locker rooms into the vivarium), put the subset of mice that you want to euthanize in the clean cage, and return the survivors to their rack in their original cage.
  • When moving live mice, pick them up by their tails.
  • There should be no more than five mice in a cage at any time, including during euthanasia.
  • Place the cage of mice that you want to euthanize in the hood.Turn the hood on (press the “flow on” button and then the “light on” button).
  • Remove the cage lid and the feeding grate and place them next to the cage in the hood.Place the metal cage lid with a port for CO2 on top of the cage.
  • Open the CO2 valve (inside the hood).
  • Turn the dial to 2.1 (or whatever is indicated on the sign above the valve on the wall next to the hood).
  • Set the timer for 5 minutes.
  • While you’re waiting, remove the cage tag. The tear-off portion at the top needs to be put into the folder on the back of the door in the housing room. This tells the vivarium to stop charging us per-diem (because the cage of mice is no longer in the housing room).
  • Grab a body bag from the drawer in the procedure room.Place the cage tag (without tear-off portion at the top) inside the bag.
  • Remember that gas flow must be maintained for at least 1 minute after clinical death. Watch for breathing and twitching. Once this stops, give the mice at least 1 more minute in the chamber. Note: Euthanization can sometimes take longer than 5 minutes!
  • Once mice are dead, remove the metal cage lid.
  • Lift the mice by their tails and check for signs of life (i.e., feel for heartbeat, or lightly pinch the foot to look for reflexes). If no signs of life are observed, place the mice into the body bag, along with the cage tag.
  • Once all mice are in the body bag, hold the bag up to the CO2 port in the metal lid to fill the bag with CO2. This is a secondary method of euthanasia - if the mice wake up, they will be in a bag full of CO2 and will die.
  • Once the bag is full of CO2, tie it off.
  • Clidox all surfaces thoroughly.
  • Place the empty cage on the “dirty cage rack”.
  • Place the bag of mice inside your cooler in the ante room.
  • Proceed back the way you came, through the locker room. Change into street clothes.
  • Grab the cooler from the ante room and leave the vivarium with your mice.
